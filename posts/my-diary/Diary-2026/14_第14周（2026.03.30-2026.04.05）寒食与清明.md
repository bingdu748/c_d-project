# 第14周（2026.03.30-2026.04.05）寒食与清明
- As 清明节 arrives, I happened to learn about the 寒食节. Curious about how it relates to 清明节, I decided to do some research.
寒食节 actually predates 清明节. It originated in 春秋时期 and is said to commemorate 介子推, a loyal minister of 晋国. When 晋文公 spent nineteen years in exile, 介子推 stayed unwaveringly by his side, even “cutting flesh from his own thigh to feed his starving lord.” Yet, after 晋文公 reclaimed the throne and began rewarding those who had served him, 介子推 was the one person he overlooked. Unwilling to compete for recognition or rewards, 介子推 withdrew to 绵山 with his mother. Overcome with regret, 晋文公 ordered the mountain to be set on fire to force him out, but 介子推 and his mother chose death over surrender, ultimately dying as they clung to a tree. Grief-stricken, 晋文公 decreed that on the anniversary of 介子推’s death, all fires throughout the land be extinguished for three days, and that people eat only cold food as an act of mourning. This became the origin of 寒食节. At first, its core customs were simply the prohibition of fire and the eating of cold food. Over time, practices such as 扫墓, 踏青, and 荡秋千 were gradually incorporated, making it one of the most important sacrificial festivals in ancient times.
清明节, on the other hand, started out as one of the 二十四节气, signaling the arrival of 仲春时节. It marked a time of renewal, when the sky was clear and the earth was bright — perfect for spring plowing and sowing. It was not until 唐代 that 清明节 began to be associated with 祭祀活动. Because 寒食节 falls just one or two days before 清明节, ancient people often extended their 扫墓活动 from 寒食节 into 清明节. In time, the 扫墓祭祖习俗 that had belonged to 寒食节 gradually shifted to 清明节.
During 唐宋时期, the integration of the two festivals reached its most defining stage. The 唐代朝廷 designated 寒食节 as an official national holiday, granting a seven-day break. People spent these days 扫墓, 踏青, and feasting, creating a lively festive atmosphere. By 宋代时期, the status of 清明节 had risen even higher, and many customs that had once belonged to 寒食节 — such as 插柳, 放风筝, and 吃青团 — were gradually absorbed by 清明节. At the same time, the 寒食节 practices of fire prohibition and eating cold food became increasingly simplified, shrinking from the original three-day ban to just one day, or even to a merely symbolic taste of cold food.
明清以后, 寒食节 gradually faded into obscurity and was eventually replaced by 清明节. Today, many people are barely aware of 寒食节, yet its essence has long been woven into the very fabric of 清明节. The 青团 and 艾饺 we enjoy during 清明节 were originally cold foods from 寒食节; the customs of 扫墓 and ancestor worship also trace back to 寒食节. It is fair to say that without 寒食节, 清明节 would not carry the rich cultural depth it does today.
时至今日, in 山西介休 — the hometown of 介子推 — people still preserve the tradition of celebrating 寒食节. Every year on the day before 清明节, every household there observes 禁火, eats 寒食, and holds various commemorative activities. In most parts, however, 寒食节 and 清明节 have long since 合二为一, becoming a single festival that combines both 自然节气 and humanistic connotations.
The merging of 寒食节 into 清明节 is a vivid reflection of the inclusiveness of traditional Chinese culture. It reminds us that festivals are never static; they constantly absorb, blend, and evolve over time. Yet, no matter how the outward forms may change, 对先人的缅怀 and 对生命的敬畏 remain the spiritual heart of these spring observances. On this day when grass grows and orioles dart through the air, we 扫墓祭祖 and 踏青赏春 — a dialogue with the past and a hope for the future.
